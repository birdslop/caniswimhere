How To Use

Search for a location using the bar above, or click anywhere on the map to search nearby. Click any map icon to see detailed water quality for that spot.

Map Icons

  • Designated Bathing Water — Officially monitored. Includes daily seasonal forecasts where available.
  • Swim / Recreation Spot — Popular swimming locations. Unofficial sites are marked accordingly.
  • Storm Overflow — Combined sewer overflows. Shows live discharge status (near-real-time) and 2024 annual spill counts. Note: EDM monitors detect overflow activation — they do not measure volume or confirm that untreated sewage reached the watercourse. False activations can occur.
  • Water Quality Sampling Point — EA WIMS lab results for bacteria, nutrients, etc.
  • EA Monitoring Station — River level, flow, and rainfall gauges.

Detail View

Click any icon or result row to open the detail panel. Water Quality appears first, followed by Live Overflow Status (showing if nearby overflows are currently discharging), overflow history, recent rainfall, and bathing water status. A gold-highlighted icon on the map shows which location is selected.

About Can I Swim Here?

Can I Swim Here? helps you make informed decisions about wild swimming and water recreation across England, Wales and Scotland. It combines multiple official data sources into a single, easy-to-read traffic-light verdict for any location. It also contains a useful Data Explorer for journalists, researchers and campaigners to use.

Verdicts

Each location receives a verdict based on the latest available water quality readings, nearby storm overflow activity, recent rainfall, and official bathing water assessments:

  • Good to swim ✓ Water quality is good, no active overflows nearby.
  • Use caution ! Elevated bacteria, recent rainfall, or nearby overflow activity.
  • Not advised ✗ Poor water quality or active discharge detected.
  • No data Insufficient or outdated data available.

When data is old or unavailable, the verdict is downgraded and a warning is shown — we never present stale data as if it were current.

Data Sources

All data comes from official, publicly available sources across England, Wales and Scotland:

  • WIMS Environment Agency Water Information Management System — laboratory-tested water quality samples including E. coli and Enterococci levels (England).
  • EDM Event Duration Monitoring — 2024 annual return with storm overflow spill counts and durations for England and Wales (Environment Agency / Natural Resources Wales). EDM monitors detect when an overflow activates — they do not measure the volume of discharge or confirm that untreated sewage entered the watercourse. Some activations may be false positives caused by sensor faults, and monitors that were offline during part of the year may undercount spills.
  • NSOH National Storm Overflow Hub — near-real-time discharge status from all nine English water companies and Scottish Water. "Discharging" means the monitor has detected overflow activation; it does not confirm volume, composition, or environmental impact. False activations are possible. Note: Dŵr Cymru Welsh Water does not publish its live feed through a public data service, so real-time status is not available for Welsh overflows.
  • SEPA Scottish Environment Protection Agency — bathing water classifications for Scotland.
  • NRW Natural Resources Wales — Welsh bathing water sites and compliance classifications.
  • EA Flood Environment Agency Flood Monitoring — real-time rainfall and river level data from gauge stations across England.
  • BWD Bathing Water Directive — official classifications, daily risk predictions, and seasonal assessments for designated bathing waters.
  • Swim Spots Recreation and swimming locations sourced from open datasets and community contributions.

Data Collection Cycles

Where more recent or real-time data is available — such as live overflow status, rainfall, and river levels — we always show it. The verdicts combine the freshest data from every source and flag when any input is stale.

Several of the datasets behind this tool are published on an annual cycle or collected only during part of the year, which is why you will often see data that stops at 2024:

  • Live overflow status (NSOH) — Near-real-time discharge monitoring only became publicly available in January 2025, so there is no live-status history before that date.
  • EDM annual returns — Water companies report storm overflow spill counts once per year. The 2024 return is the most recent available; the 2025 figures will not be published until early 2026 and will be updated then.
  • Water quality sampling (WIMS) — The Environment Agency collects bathing-water samples mainly during the official bathing season (May–September). Outside this window, months can pass with no new lab results for a given site.
  • Bathing water classifications — Official Excellent / Good / Sufficient / Poor ratings are calculated from up to four years of samples and updated once a year, typically in the autumn following the season.

Limitations

This tool is for informational purposes only and does not replace official health or safety advice. Water quality lab samples are taken periodically (often only during bathing season, May–September) and may not reflect current conditions. Real-time overflow monitoring and rainfall data help fill gaps, but local conditions can change rapidly. Always use your own judgement and check for local signage before entering the water.

Wales live overflow gap: Dŵr Cymru Welsh Water operates a near-real-time storm overflow map on their corporate website, but unlike every other water company the underlying data is served through a proprietary, non-public system. We cannot integrate their live discharge status until they publish an open data feed. Annual spill data for Welsh overflows is still included from the 2024 EDM return.

Scotland overflow data: Scottish Water publishes monitored overflow locations with live status, but does not yet provide EDM-style annual spill counts through an open dataset. Scottish overflow records show locations and live status only.

Methodology & Corrections

Live discharge status is collected via the National Storm Overflow Hub and updates throughout the day. A "Discharging" status means the EDM monitor has detected overflow activation — it does not measure volume, confirm that untreated sewage reached the watercourse, or indicate environmental impact. False activations can occur due to sensor faults. Annual spill counts come from the 2024 Event Duration Monitoring (EDM) return; monitors that were offline for part of the year may undercount events (see the edm_operational_pct field in the API). If you spot an error, please email caniswimhere@proton.me. Substantive corrections will be made promptly and noted transparently.
